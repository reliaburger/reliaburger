# 0.4.0: Planned maintenance

25 November 2026, against `main` at
[`e7c2b52d`](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/tree/e7c2b52d7) and the
0.3.3 train (`release-1-3-3`, formats 61/76). A draft for the maintainer to
review: the scope, the order, how the format bump is settled, what proves each
piece, and the questions left. Status lives in the
[0.4.0 milestone](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/milestone/7); this
plan doesn't track it.

The scope comes from the [0.4.x review](2026-11-19-review-0.4.x.md), which the
maintainer settled on 19 November 2026, and the
[mount isolation plan](2026-11-24-plan-host-mount-isolation.md), settled on
24 November. The mechanisms (move records, fencing, volume transfer) are
designed in the [move plan](2026-10-09-plan-container-migration.md) and its
[research](2026-09-28-research-container-migration.md), which haven't yet been
rewritten for the review. Where they disagree, the review and this plan win.

## The headline

**Take a node down without taking its apps down.**

0.3.0 gave every appliance cluster an OS that updates itself every week, and
so a reboot of every node every week. The rollout cordons a node, waits up to
five minutes (`DRAIN_TIMEOUT` in `src/os/rollout.rs`) for the scheduler to
re-place what it can, then reboots anyway. Replicated apps mostly survive
that. A PostgreSQL with a managed volume goes down with its node and comes
back with it, and there's no `relish drain` for the operator who wants to do
the same thing by hand.

0.4.0 is that missing verb, done properly: one cordon, a drain that respects
each app's budget and stop hooks, cold moves that carry a volume to another
node, and the OS rollout waiting on all of it. It's the cheapest and most
missed feature on the 0.4.x list, and it's useful to every cluster on the day
it ships. Checkpoint moves (CRIU) aren't in it: they went to Later on
19 November, and VMs keep memory across a move in 0.4.5.

**Exit test.** A rolling OS update of a three-node appliance cluster, under
load: a volume app keeps every acknowledged write and moves once, replicated
apps never drop below their availability budget, and no instance dies without
its stop hook.

## Order

The headline is one chain. Each link needs the one before it, and the riders
run beside it on their own.

```text
#2106 cordon record ──┐
#1776 rollout budget ─┼──► #656 drain ──► #657 cold moves ──► #659 drain by policy ──► #661 rollout ──► exit test
#1309 stop path ──────┘                     ├──► #2121 volume retirement
                                            └──► #2107 proposals (drain and uncordon after #656, move after #657)
```

1. **Open the train**, once 0.3.3's gate PR
   ([#2263](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/pull/2263)) is in
   `main`: version 0.4.0 and the format bump in its first commit.
2. **The riders land first**, since they're written: #2283, #2301 (once #2294
   is in `main` with 0.3.3), #2284, #2297, and #2282 after its Wyse check.
   That clears five of the train's conflicts before the long work starts.
3. **The cordon record** (#2106), with #1776 and #1309 in parallel. #2106 has
   to trace whether task-array grants honour a cordon (the review couldn't
   say), because a drain that doesn't stop grants never finishes.
4. **Drain** (#656), on the record and the budget. Its planner's property
   test, "never below an app's availability budget", is the release's first
   real invariant.
5. **Cold moves** (#657), starting with S12's fencing. That's the release's
   one go or no-go: if the property test can't hold with `main`'s fences, we
   stop and redesign, and 0.4.0 ships drain alone with the exit test
   changed to match. Then plain directories, then loop-ext4 and Btrfs.
   Volume retirement (#2121) follows the first volume move, since every move
   leaves a tombstone.
6. **Drain by move policy** (#659): the policy table, logs and metrics across
   a move, the recovery profile, and the soak's new fault kinds. The fault
   kinds go in as early as they can, so harness artefacts show up on the
   train and not in the gate.
7. **The rollout** (#661) swaps its five-minute wait for the drain.
8. **Proposals** (#2107) whenever drain and moves have settled their shapes,
   on a parallel agent.
9. **Export and import** (#2108) any time; it touches neither the scheduler
   nor the rollout.

## Formats: one bump for the release

`CURRENT` in `src/compatibility.rs` is 61/76 on `main` and on the 0.3.3
train. Two drafts each took their own numbers: #2283 62/77 (the owner record
schema and a renamed gossip field), and #2301 63/78 (a field on
`JobDefinition` and `DeploymentRecord`, and two Raft writes). The headline
adds more: the cordon record, `MoveRecord`, the rollout budget, new proposal
kinds and a node lease, and `[app.x.move]` in `AppSpec`.

None of that needs a number of its own. No 0.4.0 cluster exists between two
PRs on the train, so we follow 0.2.1's rule:

- **One bump, 62/77, in the train's first commit**, under a "0.4.0, planned
  maintenance (62/77)" heading that says each later change adds its line, not
  another bump. #2283 keeps its numbers. #2301 drops to 62/77, and its line
  joins the heading.
- **Every format change adds its line** under that heading in the same PR, and
  regenerates the fuzz seeds (`make fuzz-corpus-seed`), as #1941 asks.
- **`make test-upgrade` runs on every PR that adds a line**, so the refusal of
  61/76 peers and state is checked as the formats grow.
- **A closing bump only if a train build escaped.** 0.3.0 had to bump a second
  time at freeze, because nightly builds and a lab image made mid-train spoke
  the first bump's numbers with a different wire. If any 0.4.0 build leaves the
  Studio before freeze, we take 63/78 at freeze, with a line saying why
  (question 2).

0.4.0 therefore starts every cluster afresh: a 0.3.x cluster can't roll onto
it, which is what #2108's export and import are for.

**The gate is the full one.** 0.4.0 is a minor release, it changes `CURRENT`,
and the drain and rollout work will almost certainly touch the agent loop, so
`release_tier.py` will say `full` three times over
([a gate scaled to the release](../releasing.md#a-gate-scaled-to-the-release)):
the staged installs, the V02 and jobs fast tiers, the power-cut and reboot
runs, and the 8-hour jobs final, with moves and drains now in the schedule.
On top of that, in `docs/qualification/`:

- **the exit test's record**: the rolling OS update of three appliance nodes,
  with the volume app's write ledger, the budget's lowest point per app, and
  every instance's hook outcome (question 4 says on what hardware);
- **the laptop demo**: drain a quickstart node running a volume app and a
  replicated one, and the recovery profile, once on Apple silicon and once on
  Linux;
- **the hollow-node record** at 500 nodes, updated by #2284.

The soak changes its own schedule in this release, and harness changes have
cost us before (0.2.0 passed on two documented harness artefacts). So the new
fault kinds land early and run in the train's nightly qualification, and
the gate starts only after a clean fast tier with them in.

## The book

Most of 0.4.0 is new subsystem, so it gets a new chapter: **`20-moving-day.md`,
"Moving Day"**, the working title the move plan gave it, written PR by PR. It
follows a node through a weekly OS update: why three cordons became one, how
the drain planner spends a budget it shares with deploys, the move record as a
state machine whose activation is a one-way door, carrying a volume without
ever having two homes or none, and the rollout that waits for all of it. The
Rust it can teach on first use: proptest's state-machine testing (S12's
model), `enum` transitions that make "restore the source after activation"
unrepresentable, and streaming a large transfer with incremental hashing (the
Pickle pull it reuses). `CLAUDE.md`'s chapter table gets its row in the PR
that creates the file.

## Risks

- **S12 says no.** Then there are no moves in 0.4.0, and drain blocks every
  volume app by name. The release would still be worth shipping, but its exit
  test would shrink to "the rollout waits on a drain and every volume app is
  named", and the headline gets a lot quieter. We find out in the first weeks
  of #657, not at the gate.
- **A move that loses data.** The import's crash windows, Btrfs incremental
  sends and loop-ext4 quotas are where a cold move can go wrong quietly. The
  crash tests between every step and the write ledger are the defence;
  "verified deltas, never size and mtime alone" is in #657 for a reason.
- **Room on small nodes.** A Wyse has about 1 GB of memory for workloads and a
  small data partition. A cold move needs room for the volume on the target,
  and a rollout that drains one node of three puts its apps on two. The
  planner has to refuse what won't fit, by name, rather than start a move it
  can't finish.
- **Stop hooks against the clock.** A drain that honours a long `stop_grace`
  meets the rollout's 45-minute timeout and the service manager's stop bound.
  Both limits need writing down beside the hook rules.
- **The agent loop.** Drain, cordon and move handling add work to Bun's loop
  and to the leader's. Slow journal turns have shipped as known issues before
  ([#1707](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/1707) in 0.2.7),
  and the `watch`-borrow rule in `CLAUDE.md` applies to every new reader of
  Raft metrics and the directory.
- **The crate split.** The [split plan](2026-11-08-plan-crate-split.md) wanted
  to land before 0.4.0's trains. It hasn't started. If it lands mid-train, every
  open 0.4.0 PR conflicts with it (question 1).
- **Format numbers escaping the train.** The 0.3.0 precedent: builds made
  between the first bump and freeze spoke the same numbers with a different
  wire.
- **Stale issue bodies.** #659 and #661 still describe CRIU work, and #661
  says it's 0.4.1. An agent working from them would build the wrong thing
  (question 8).
- **Mount isolation changes host workloads.** A host log shipper reading Bun's
  directories, or a workload that mounts, traces or loads BPF, breaks on
  default Linux nodes. It's the point, but it goes in the release notes.
- **The Studio.** Hollow-node runs, power-cut endurance, the recovery profile
  and the gate all want the Studio, and soaks need it quiet. Long runs get
  their own worktrees and their slots are booked around the gate.

## Questions for the maintainer

1. **Does the crate split go first?** The review said it should land before
   0.4.0's trains; it hasn't started, and five 0.4.0 drafts are written against
   the single crate. *Recommendation:* don't hold 0.4.0 for it. Run the split
   in the quiet window after 0.4.0 freezes, when no train has open PRs to
   conflict with.
2. **How is the format bump settled?** *Recommendation:* one bump to 62/77 in
   the train's first commit, with #2301 renumbered from 63/78 and every other
   change adding a line under the 0.4.0 heading. Publish no nightly or lab
   build from the train: its nightly qualification runs on the Studio
   without staging to the nightly channel. If a build does escape, take 63/78
   at freeze, as 0.3.0 did.
3. **What does a drain do with a volume app that has no `[app.x.move]`
   table?** *Recommendation:* move it cold by default, since that's what the
   headline promises, with an explicit "stay" that blocks the drain by name
   (and, in an OS rollout, goes down with the node as it does today).
   Host-path apps always block, and `--force` stops them with their data left
   in place.
4. **On what hardware does the exit test gate the tag?** *Recommendation:* a
   three-node QEMU appliance cluster on the Studio gates it, since it's
   repeatable and scriptable. Three Wyse 3040s run the same test on the
   release's artefacts in parallel and get their own record, without holding
   the tag, as #407 did for 0.3.0.
5. **#1967 shipped on the 0.3.3 train.** *Recommendation:* confirm it's out of
   0.4.0, and drop it from the roadmap's 0.4.0 entry when this plan merges.
6. **#2301 does item 1 of #1322, which sits in 0.3.3.** If nothing
   changes, the 0.4.0 train closes an issue whose other items 0.3.3 is
   handling. *Recommendation:* split item 1 into its own 0.4.0 issue and point
   #2301 at that, leaving #1322 with 0.3.3.
7. **Does the export half of #2108 ride 0.3.3?** It's read-only and changes no
   format, and it has to exist on the cluster you're leaving. Shipped in 0.4.0,
   the first crossing it helps is 0.4.0 to 0.4.1; shipped in 0.3.3, it covers
   the crossing into 0.4.0, the one every appliance cluster will face first.
   *Recommendation:* the export rides 0.3.3 if its freeze allows, otherwise a
   0.3.4 patch; the import lands on the 0.4.0 train.
8. **Rewrite #659 and #661 to this plan?** Both bodies predate the review.
   *Recommendation:* yes, once this plan is approved. #659 loses the Redis
   memory demo, the job-task move and checkpoint modes, and keeps the move
   policy, logs and metrics across moves, the recovery profile and the soak's
   fault kinds. #661 says 0.4.0, drops #660, and moves PostgreSQL by cold move.
   #1309 drops its Apple stop test, which goes to Later with the runtime.
9. **Does the bun upgrade drain?** The review has both the OS rollout and the
   bun upgrade waiting on a real drain. But a bun upgrade keeps its workloads
   running across the `exec` (chapter 14), so a drain would stop apps that
   didn't need stopping. *Recommendation:* the bun upgrade writes the shared
   cordon record and doesn't drain; only reboots drain.
10. **Is a 25-node soak part of 0.4.0?** The review paired #1411 with one.
    *Recommendation:* #1411 is proved by `make test-hollow` and the hollow
    record at 500. Run a real 25-node cluster on the Studio once before freeze
    and publish the record, outside the gate.
11. **What slips if 0.4.0 runs long?** *Recommendation:* the release is
    #2106, #656, #1776, #1309, #657, #2121 and #661's rollout. Proposals
    (#2107), the `--profile move` conformance suite and the import half of
    #2108 can ride 0.4.1 beside the front door without changing 0.4.0's
    headline.

## Done when

Every issue above is closed by a merged PR or moved by a recorded decision,
the exit test passes and is recorded, the full gate passes on a candidate
built from `main`, and the maintainer tags and promotes 0.4.0. Releases go out
when they're ready and verified, with no target date.
