# Plan: certificate automation (ACME) for the ingress, F08

[#1667](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/1667), milestone 0.4.0. Planned on 6 November 2026 as part of [#369](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/369) for 0.3.0. The same day, the maintainer moved ACME out of 0.3.0 into its own issue. The other two parts of F08, the WebSocket Close 1001 on a drain deadline and the proxy-owned `X-Real-IP` and `X-Request-ID` on WebSocket upgrades, shipped in 0.3.0 with this plan in the same PR. Bare-metal clusters face real networks, and today an app that wants a publicly trusted certificate has to bring its own (`tls = "explicit"`). `auto` and `acme` are refused by `relish apply` and `relish lint` ([ingress design](../design/ingress-wrapper.md), §5.3).

## What it has to do

An app sets `[app.web.ingress] host = "shop.example.com"` and `tls = "acme"`. The cluster gets a certificate for that name from an ACME CA (Let's Encrypt by default), serves it on every node's ingress, renews it well before it expires, and says so in `relish wtf` when renewal is failing. Nothing on the operator's laptop is involved after the apply.

## Decisions to make first

1. **Challenge type: HTTP-01 only, at first.** Every node already listens on port 80, and the CA can reach whichever node the name's DNS points at. TLS-ALPN-01 needs the TLS listener to answer a special ALPN on 443, which is more code in the hot path. DNS-01 needs a DNS provider API per provider, so it's out until someone asks for wildcards.
2. **Who talks to the CA: the leader.** One ACME account per cluster, its key wrapped like every other key in council state. The leader places orders, and every node serves the challenge.
3. **How every node serves the challenge: through Raft.** The CA may fetch `http://<host>/.well-known/acme-challenge/<token>` from any node DNS names. The leader writes the pending key authorisation into desired state (`AcmeChallenge { token, key_authorization, expires }`), each node's ingress answers that one path from its replica, and the leader only tells the CA to validate once a quorum has applied it. That's the same "commit, then act" order the CA rotation uses. Today port 80 deliberately has no ACME exception ([ingress design](../design/ingress-wrapper.md), port 80), so this adds exactly one: a path whose token is in desired state, and nothing else.
4. **Where the certificate lives: council state.** The certificate chain and its private key (wrapped) go into desired state beside the cluster-issued ingress certificates. Nodes load them as they load those today, so a new node serves the right certificate without asking anyone.
5. **When to renew: ARI where the CA offers it, else two-thirds of the way through.** Let's Encrypt asks clients to use ACME Renewal Information and, without it, to renew about two-thirds of the way through a certificate's life rather than on a fixed cycle. Its default lifetime goes from 90 days to 64 on 10 February 2027 and to 45 on 16 February 2028 ([Let's Encrypt, certificate lifetimes](https://letsencrypt.org/docs/cert-lifetimes/)), and the CA/Browser Forum caps public certificates at 47 days from 15 March 2029. So nothing may assume 90 days.
6. **The client library: [instant-acme](https://github.com/djc/instant-acme).** It's pure Rust on tokio and rustls, implements [RFC 8555](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8555), serialises account credentials so they can live in council state, and supports key rollover and revocation ([README](https://docs.rs/crate/instant-acme/latest/source/README.md)). Two limits to check before committing to it: its README says it has been tested with DNS challenges only, and account keys are P-256 ECDSA only. Both are fine for HTTP-01 with P-256, but the first means our tests carry the HTTP-01 path.
7. **Which CA by default:** Let's Encrypt production, with `[ingress] acme_directory` to name staging or another CA, and `acme_contact` for the account email. A cluster that sets no contact gets no account, and `tls = "acme"` is refused at apply with a message saying what to set.

## Failure behaviour

- **The first order fails** (DNS not pointing at the cluster, port 80 blocked): the route serves the cluster-issued certificate meanwhile, so the site works with a browser warning rather than not at all, and `relish wtf` names the host and the CA's error.
- **A renewal fails:** keep serving the current certificate and retry with backoff. `relish wtf` warns once a third of the remaining life is gone, and goes critical at seven days left.
- **Rate limits:** one order per host at a time, and backoff that honours the CA's `Retry-After`. Let's Encrypt's limits make a retry storm across a fleet expensive.
- **A leader change mid-order:** the order URL and challenge are in desired state, so the new leader carries on or starts afresh. A half-finished order expires on the CA's side by itself.

## Tests first

- **Unit:**
  - the renewal schedule (ARI window, two-thirds fallback, 45- and 64-day lifetimes);
  - the challenge route answers only tokens in desired state, and only on port 80;
  - an `acme` route without `acme_contact` is refused at apply.
- **Integration, on every PR:** [Pebble](https://github.com/letsencrypt/pebble), Let's Encrypt's test CA, in a container. A three-node in-process cluster issues a certificate for a test name, serves it on all three nodes, and renews it under live traffic with no failed requests. That's F08's completion test: "renewal under live traffic".
- **Gated:** the same against Let's Encrypt staging on the appliance lab, once a Wyse cluster has a public name.

## Compatibility and docs

- **Compatibility:** desired state gains the ACME account, pending challenges and issued certificates, so the change bumps `state` (and `protocol` for the challenge entry), under the 0.3.0 heading in `src/compatibility.rs`.
- **Docs, in the same PR:** manual chapter 3 ("TLS"), the ingress design doc §5.3 (from *deferred design* to what ships), book chapter 3, and `relish wtf`'s check list.

## Order

1. The account and the challenge route, with Pebble in CI.
2. Issuance and serving on every node.
3. Renewal, ARI, and the `relish wtf` checks.
4. The appliance-lab run against staging.

About a week of work. It can land after the Wyse exit test without holding it up, since nothing in the exit test needs a public name.
