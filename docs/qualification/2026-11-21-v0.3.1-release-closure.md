# 0.3.1 release closure

21 November 2026. How the 0.3.1 candidate was qualified: what each gate run
found, and what follows from it. The per-run evidence lives in the records
this page links; this page is the map.

> **Candidate 1 (`1612ee38`) is the release candidate.** It passed both
> staged installs, the power-cut and reboot lanes (25 of 25 in every lane),
> the V02 fast tier and the jobs fast tier, all clean and all at the first
> attempt. The 8-hour jobs final ran its whole schedule (10 h 15 min: eight
> cycles, four upgrade walks, both quorum losses, the all-off and a quiet
> tail with every leak checker armed on every node, all ok; council history
> linearizable; no data lost) with two failures from one cause: after a
> chaos pause and then a chaos drop, the cluster didn't count as settled
> within 300 s (#2197).
>
> **Known issue it ships with:** #2197, accepted by the maintainer on
> 21 November 2026. `relish wtf` counts a crash loop per app, not per
> instance, so three replicas that each restarted once read as a critical
> `crashloop` for 15 minutes, and the soak's settle check waits for no
> critical finding. 0.3.1's fix to Smoker's pause (#2079) made the pause
> freeze the workload itself, so its health checks failed and Bun restarted
> it, correctly; that third restart tipped `frontend` over the line. The
> rule is unchanged since 0.3.0. Chaos pause and chaos drop settled cleanly
> in both fast tiers (16 to 33 s). The fix (#2198) is on the 0.3.2 train.

## The gate

`scripts/release/release_tier.py` asked for the full gate: 0.3.1 changes the
agent loop (`src/bun/agent*`: #2140's exec deadline, #2153 and #2133), and
any change there gets the 8-hour jobs final. The wire format and durable
state are unchanged from 0.3.0 (protocol 60, state 76), so a rolling upgrade
works. Every tier ran on the candidate itself, on the Mac Studio, with
nothing else built or tested there while it ran; the power-cut endurance run
(#2067) paused itself for the gate.

The final's record says FAIL and lists chaos pause and chaos drop as missing
from its execution inventory: a fault counts there only once the cluster
settled after it, and those two are the settles that timed out. Every other
fault class settled every time, the slowest in 65 s. One agent-loop turn on
node 1 ran 268 ms past its deadline at 18:02, in a journal write during
`consumer_sync` while the disk was busy with `fsync`; the stall watchdog
logged it and the loop went on. It isn't a gated check.
