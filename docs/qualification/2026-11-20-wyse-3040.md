# Wyse 3040 lab: "Bare metal in an hour"

20 to 23 November 2026. The appliance's exit test on real hardware: Dell
Wyse 3040 thin clients netbooted from a MacBook Pro, claimed over the LAN
into a three-node cluster, and measured for 24 hours. It's the record the
[Wyse lab checklist](../plans/2026-11-06-wyse-lab-checklist.md) asks for,
and the evidence for [#404](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/404)
(`relish netboot`), [#405](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/405)
(claiming machines over the LAN) and
[#407](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/407) (the exit test).

> **Status: done on three Wyses, with two misses carried into 0.3.3.**
> Phases 1 and 2 ran on 0.3.0's lab build (20–21 November). Phase 3 redid
> the whole lab on the released 0.3.2 on 23 November: a fresh install and
> claim of all three units, the cord tests, [#286](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/286)'s
> live catalogue, the cross-node firewall, the broken-update fallback and a
> cluster OS update. The maintainer decided on 23 November that three Wyses
> are enough for the exit test. The fallback still misses 10 minutes
> ([#2143](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2143),
> [#2245](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2245)) and the
> eMMC write rate misses its threshold
> ([#2201](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2201)).
> [#287](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/287)'s Btrfs power
> cuts ran in disposable Lima VMs instead (#2227), not on the Wyses.

Log lines quoted here have had machine identities (MACs, UUIDs, claim keys)
and timestamps taken out.

## Setup

- **Hardware:** three Dell Wyse 3040s (Atom x5-Z8350, 2 GB RAM, 16 GB eMMC),
  BIOS 1.2.3, UEFI boot, PXE on the integrated NIC, Secure Boot off. One
  arrived with Windows on an NTFS layout, one with Ubuntu on LVM, and one
  with Ubuntu and IGEL boot entries. None still had ThinOS. One unit's CMOS
  coin cell is flat, so it loses its BIOS settings and clock when unplugged.
- **Network:** setup B from the checklist. A switch holds only the Wyses and
  the Mac's USB Ethernet adapter. The Mac routes and NATs the lab out
  through its Wi-Fi, and dnsmasq hands out addresses with a reservation per
  unit. `relish netboot` answers PXE on UDP 4011 only (`--mode-dhcp-proxy`).
- **Software:** the appliance lab build of 0.3.0's candidate 4 (`efc3fb2`;
  bun reports the merge commit `d473cb1`, the same code). OS image
  2026.47.226, with 2026.47.227 as the next version and 2026.47.228 as the
  broken one. `relish` was built on the Mac from the same commit.
- **Workload:** `nginx:alpine` from Docker Hub with an ingress host, in
  place of the burger demo, which needs a build the appliance can't do
  ([#2144](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2144)).

## Go/no-go

| Criterion | Threshold | Result | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machines claimed | 3 of 3 | 3 of 3 | ✅ |
| Power-on to a working cluster | ≤ 60 min | 38 min on 0.3.0; about 25 min on 0.3.2 | ✅ |
| `MemAvailable` on every node under the workload | ≥ 1.0 GB | 1.38 GB (lowest, wyse-1) | ✅ |
| eMMC life at the measured write rate, worst node | ≥ 5 years | 2.2 years (wyse-1, 2.95 GB a day) | accepted pending [#2201](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2201) |
| A broken update falls back by itself | ≤ 10 min | 10 min 28 s on 0.3.0; 11 min 38 s on 0.3.2 | ❌ fix in 0.3.3 ([#2143](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2143), [#2245](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2245)) |

The fallback missed by 28 s on 0.3.0. The maintainer accepted it on 20
November 2026 pending #2143, which cut boot-check's wait per try from 120 s
to 90 s in 0.3.2. That should have put three tries plus the final boot at
about 9 minutes, but on 0.3.2 it took 11 min 38 s: each try now spends
about 65 s booting before boot-check even starts, because boot-check waits
for the network to come up first (Phase 3). 0.3.3 moves boot-check ahead
of the network wait (#2245) and retests it on the Wyses.

The eMMC life missed too: 2.2 years on the leader against 5. The
maintainer accepted it on 21 November 2026 pending #2201, which finds and
cuts the writes in a later release (milestone Later).

## Phase 2: three Wyses

### Install (2.2)

All three installed from one `relish netboot --reinstall` with three
`--mac` and `--wipe` flags. With one monitor and keyboard, the units were
powered on one after another, with F12 on each. T0 is the first unit's
power-on.

- The units' eMMC showed up as `/dev/mmcblk0` on one and `/dev/mmcblk1` on
  another. The same unit used one name during the install and the other
  on a later boot. The installer picks the eMMC either way.
- **One unit hung on the installer's reboot.** relish logged `installed`,
  and the installer released its DHCP lease, but the machine never came
  back: the screen kept the last kernel lines, and Alt+F2 and Ctrl+Alt+Del
  did nothing. Holding the power button and powering it on booted the
  install normally. Later warm reboots of the same unit, after the claim,
  worked
  ([#2163](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2163)).
- **No unit showed its status on its screen before the claim.** Each stayed
  on the kernel's last boot lines (`sof-audio-acpi-intel-byt … sof_probe_work
  failed err: -2` and `intel-spi … probe with driver intel-spi failed with
  error -22`, both harmless). The console unit waits for the seed service,
  which waits until the machine is claimed
  ([#2161](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2161)).

### Claim (2.3)

```
$ relish machines claim ~/wyse --create --name wyse --operator <lab-ip> --network <net> \
    --ssh-key ~/.ssh/id_rsa.pub <mac-1> <mac-2> <mac-3>
  wyse-1 (<mac-1> at <ip-1>): claimed
  wyse-2 (<mac-2> at <ip-2>): claimed
  wyse-3 (<mac-3> at <ip-3>): claimed
cluster wyse created in ~/wyse
```

Then `relish nodes` showed three alive voters, `relish council` 3 of 3
with quorum ok, and `relish wtf` 17 OK with nothing to fix. **T1 − T0 was
38 minutes**, including about 20 minutes spent on the unit whose installer
reboot hung.

**Deviation: the claim keys weren't compared before the claim.** Because of
#2161, no unit showed its key, so relish's "Does the console show claim key
…?" couldn't be checked. Each node's key was checked afterwards, from the
key it logged while unclaimed (`journalctl -u reliaburger-seed`): two of the
three matched what `relish machines` had listed. The third couldn't be
checked, because that node refuses SSH
([#2165](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2165)). It's
alive, a voter, and its bun answers, and it's the only machine relish served
on that MAC.

One smaller finding from the claim: relish kept a stale context that
pointed at a deleted cluster, then failed with `API error (status 0): No
such file or directory`
([#2162](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2162)).

### Workload and cord test (2.4)

- `nginx:alpine` was applied, pulled and running about 30 s after the apply.
- **Cord pulled on a follower.** It showed as `dead` within seconds, quorum
  held at 2 of 3, and it was back as `alive` about 40 to 45 s after the
  power came back.
- **A clean power-off of another follower** (its power button) made it
  disappear from `relish nodes` rather than show it as down, while
  `relish council` still counted it
  ([#2164](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2164)). It
  rejoined after the power came back.
- **A warm reboot** (Ctrl+Alt+Del) of the unit whose installer reboot had
  hung worked: it was back about a minute later.
- About 30 minutes in, the built-in `cpu_idle` alert fired on the two idle
  nodes and turned the cluster `DEGRADED`
  ([#2171](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2171)).

### Measurement (2.5)

`image/lab/fleet-measure.sh` samples every 5 minutes for 24 hours, from
21:37 GMT on 20 November. nginx runs for the first 12 hours and is deleted
at 09:45 GMT on 21 November. The node without SSH (#2165) isn't sampled.
First samples:

| Node | `MemAvailable` | bun RSS | Data used | Temperature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wyse-1 (leader, runs nginx) | 1.40 GB | 206 MB | 165 MB of 12.8 GB | 51 °C |
| wyse-3 | 1.52 GB | 106 MB | 129 MB of 12.8 GB | 47 °C |

The run ended at 21:37 GMT on 21 November. It has two gaps with no
samples: 08:28 to 09:31, when the Mac's disk filled and the sampler died,
and 16:48 to 17:15, when the session running it restarted. The write
counter is cumulative and neither node rebooted, so the gaps don't change
the eMMC figures; the memory minimum can't see the two missing stretches.

| Node | Lowest `MemAvailable`, first 12 h | bun RSS, highest | Data used, end | Temperature, highest | Swap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wyse-1 | 1.38 GB | 213 MB | 198 MB | 53 °C | none |
| wyse-3 | 1.50 GB | 113 MB | 149 MB | 55 °C | none |

Neither node's `MemAvailable` went lower in the idle second half.

Life is years to 300 × 8 GB written at the measured rate, the plan's
measure; five years is about 1.3 GB a day.

| Node | eMMC written a day, 24 h | with nginx (first 12 h) | idle (after nginx) | Life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wyse-1 (leader) | 2.95 GB | 3.26 GB | 2.57 GB | 2.2 years ❌ |
| wyse-3 | 1.72 GB | 1.69 GB | 1.75 GB | 3.8 years ❌ |

Deleting nginx barely changed the rate, so the writes are the platform's
own, and the leader writes about twice what a follower does. All of them
land on the Btrfs root. By process, after about 25 hours of uptime, bun
wrote 789 MB on wyse-1 and 403 MB on wyse-3, `btrfs-transaction` 579 MB and
338 MB, and journald about 145 MB on each. About half of the leader's
device writes aren't attributed to a live process: Btrfs's copy-on-write
and metadata, or processes that have exited.
[#2201](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2201) finds and
cuts them.

## Phase 3: the whole lab again on 0.3.2

23 November 2026, on the released 0.3.2 (bun `2aae7e2`, OS image
2026.47.270, with 2026.47.271 as the next version and 2026.47.272 as the
broken one). Same network as Phase 2. The unit with the flat coin cell was
retired and replaced by another 3040 with a good one, so any unit could be
power-cycled.

### Install and claim

All three units were wiped and reinstalled from one `relish netboot
--reinstall`, then claimed together.

- The install took about 30 s per unit. From power-on to the claim key on
  the unit's screen took **2 min 53 s** by stopwatch.
- **Every unit showed its claim key on screen** (#2161), and each key
  matched what `relish machines` listed before the claim, closing
  [#405](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/405)'s check.
- **T1 − T0 was about 25½ minutes** for three units with one monitor and
  keyboard, including the BIOS setup on the replacement unit.
- SSH worked on all three nodes, and no installer reboot hung, so neither
  [#2165](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2165) nor
  [#2163](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2163)
  reproduced. Both stay open as unconfirmed.

### Workload and cord tests

`nginx:alpine` with an ingress host was running 27 s after the apply.

| Cord pulled on | What happened |
|---|---|
| A follower | Showed `dead`, quorum held, back as `alive` 1 min 17 s after the power came back. |
| The leader | Another node took over as leader. relish couldn't reach the cluster until the old leader came back, about 1 min 32 s after the power did, because its context names only one node ([#2241](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2241)). |
| The node running nginx | About 25 s without service: the ingress went from no answer to `502` to `200`, with nginx rescheduled on another node. |

### Cross-node firewall

A target app on one node with `allow_from` listing two callers, and the
callers forced onto the other two nodes. Both listed callers reached the
target's service address, an unlisted app got `EPERM`, and neither caller
needed a published port.

### Live catalogue (#286)

`relish test --profile full-runc`, then `relish test --chaos`, against the
three-node cluster:

| Run | Cases | Pass | Fail | Skipped | Unknown | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Integration | 41 | 22 | 0 | 18 | 1 | 2 min 44 s |
| Chaos | 5 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 min 6 s |

- The 18 skipped cases need the process runtime, which the appliance
  doesn't offer.
- Integration's unknown is `volume_size_limit_is_enforced`: size limits
  need a loop mount, which the appliance doesn't set up.
- Chaos's unknown is `node_death_during_deploy_ends_clean`: the rolling
  deploy finished before the case could kill a node during it.
- Chaos first refused to run because node pressure was unavailable on every
  node. The cgroup controllers it needs weren't on when bun started, and
  bun only checked once. Restarting bun fixed it
  ([#2242](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2242)).

### Broken-update fallback

One node was given 2026.47.272, which never passes boot-check. It tried
three times, the boot counter went from `+3-0` to `+0-3`, and it booted
2026.47.270 again by itself. It took **11 min 38 s**, against 10 minutes.

Each try took about 3 min 20 s:

| Part | Time |
|---|---|
| Firmware and boot loader | about 46 s |
| Kernel and initrd | about 21 s |
| Waiting for the network (`systemd-networkd-wait-online`) | about 23 s |
| Rest of boot, up to boot-check | about 21 s |
| boot-check's wait | 90 s |

boot-check is ordered after the boot-log streamer, which waits for the
network. The fix for #2245 moves it ahead, so a try costs the boot plus 90 s and a
node whose network never comes up still falls back.

### Cluster OS update

`relish os upgrade` to 2026.47.271 updated one node at a time and finished
on all three. Two nodes took about 3 minutes each. The third took 21
minutes: after its warm reboot, its network link never came up (no light
on the switch port). Replugging the cable didn't help; a power cycle did.
The rollout correctly waited for it. boot-check never rebooted that node,
because it was still waiting for the network
([#2245](https://github.com/reliaburger/reliaburger/issues/2245)).
