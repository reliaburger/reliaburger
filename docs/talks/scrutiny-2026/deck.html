Reliaburger · design goal

Scrutiny, not popularity

Why we ask you to trust an orchestrator for what anyone can check, not for how many people run it.

Miko Pawlikowski · November 2026 · arrow keys to move, N for notes

Open with the question: how did you decide your last piece of infrastructure was safe to run? For most of us the honest answer is "lots of people use it". This talk is about why that stopped working in 2026, and what Reliaburger does instead. Keep it under ten minutes.

The old rule

"Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow"

  • Linus's Law, as Eric Raymond put it: popular code is safe code, because someone would have noticed.
  • Heartbleed: in OpenSSL for over two years before anyone said so.
  • Log4Shell: in every Log4j from 2.0-beta9 to 2.14.1.
  • xz: caught by one engineer curious about slow ssh logins, in "an unpaid hobby project".

Sources 1–5

Lots of users is not the same as lots of reviewers. Heartbleed: shipped in OpenSSL 1.0.1 (March 2012), found and disclosed in April 2014. Log4Shell: CISA's guidance gives the affected range 2.0-beta9 to 2.14.1. xz: Andres Freund noticed ssh logins using too much CPU and valgrind errors; Russ Cox's timeline quotes Lasse Collin calling xz "an unpaid hobby project". Don't overstate it: the rule worked often enough to feel true.

2026

The CVE count went through the roof

92,319CVEs from January to October 2026, against 41,213 in the same months of 2025
17,481CVEs in October 2026 alone, against 4,389 a year earlier
2,118Linux kernel CVEs in September 2026, over a third of its whole 2025
948distinct kernel bugs with working root exploits, in one OpenAI run, for about $1.2M

Sources 6–11

All third parties' counts of public data. The 92,319 and 17,481 are RuntimeWire's count of the NVD API (1 November 2026); 2025's full year was a record 48,185 by Jerry Gamblin's count, quoted by Trend Micro. Gamblin's May forecast already had 2026 crossing 1,000 CVEs a week in March. Kernel: Linux CVE Tracker, from NVD: 5,683 in 2025, 8,311 in 2026 by 7 November. Causes are mixed: Trend Micro names AI-assisted discovery as one of three drivers, beside reporting changes and product sprawl, and in May Gamblin put AI-found CVEs under 1% of the total. The kernel's CNA gives almost every bug fix a CVE. OpenAI's post is one example inside the wave, not the wave: 2,308 candidates, 1,724 reproduced, 1,169 working local privilege-escalation exploits, 948 distinct bugs after grouping, $1,213,430 in API credits, not independently verified, not 948 CVEs. Anthropic's Glasswing scan estimated 6,202 high or critical bugs across 1,000+ projects.

So what changed

Popular code is searched hardest, by everyone

  • The same models work for both sides. Popularity now mostly means you're first in the queue.
  • What counts is how quickly a fix reaches every machine you run.
  • On Kubernetes that's many queues: the project's own feed lists 95 CVEs since 2017, and etcd, containerd, runc, Helm and Argo CD each keep their own.
  • One add-on, ingress-nginx, scored 9.8 in March 2025, and Wiz found it in about 43% of cloud environments.

Sources 16, 19

The Kubernetes count is ours: we downloaded the official JSON feed on 10 November 2026 and counted 95 entries, 13 published in 2026. The feed covers only issues the Kubernetes Security Response Committee announces; it includes subprojects such as ingress-nginx (22 entries mention it) but not etcd, containerd, runc, Helm or Argo CD. IngressNightmare is CVE-2025-1974 and three siblings, CVSS 9.8.

Reliaburger's answer

One thing to upgrade, and cheap to check

  • One thing to upgrade. Not Kubernetes, etcd, a CNI, ingress, cert-manager, Prometheus, Grafana, logs, Argo CD and a registry on a dozen release cycles: one binary. relish upgrade start rolls it, signed, with automatic rollback.
  • The OS next. 0.3.0 plans a netbooted appliance image with bun as its only service. Planned, not shipped.
  • Open, and readable by any model. Every release relish carries its exact source; continuous LLM review is a 1.0 design goal.
  • Ten times less code, in Rust.

Sources 12, 17–18

Lead with the upgrade. The pain on Kubernetes isn't one upgrade, it's a dozen release cycles and the one version you forgot. A fix in Reliaburger is a new bun, one command, workers then council then leader. The appliance image (0.3.0, "Bare metal in an hour"): our own mkosi build of Ubuntu 26.04, no SSH, no package manager, read-only verified /usr, netbooted by relish netboot, signed weekly OS builds that bun rolls out with A/B slots. Continuous LLM scrutiny is issue #1840 in the 1.0 milestone; today LLM reviews happen in audits someone starts. Rust: Google measured about a thousand times lower memory-safety vulnerability density in Android's Rust than its C and C++.

Sources 13–15

Methods are in the dated record, docs/qualification/2026-11-10-loc-comparison.md. scc 4.1.0 on both sides, code lines of the main language only, no tests, vendored, marked-generated code, docs or examples, at each project's latest release. The thirteen: Kubernetes, etcd, containerd, CoreDNS, ingress-nginx, cert-manager, Prometheus, Alertmanager, Loki, Grafana's Go backend, Argo CD, distribution, Cilium. runc and Helm are left out: Reliaburger uses runc, and has no Helm. Without Cilium and Grafana the ratio is 6.7. Lines aren't bugs, and Go is wordier than Rust. Dependencies: 698 distinct direct and 1,194 total Go modules across the thirteen go.mod files, against 66 direct and 521 crate versions. Releases: tags v0.1.0 (29 September) to v0.2.7 (7 November 2026). The planted bug is the agent gym's planted-logs-since scenario in 0.2.7's exit test, run with a read-only token.

Honest limits

What this doesn't buy you yet

  • Lines aren't bugs, and the Kubernetes side does more: without Cilium and Grafana it's 6.7×, not 10.5×.
  • Continuous LLM review is a 1.0 goal (#1840). Today it happens when someone starts an audit.
  • Until the 0.3.0 appliance image ships, the host's kernel and packages patch on their own cycle.
  • One binary is one target: a bug in bun is on every node.
  • A person starts every upgrade, and before 1.0 a format change means a fresh cluster.
Say these out loud; the audience will think of them anyway. The crates bring their own advisories too: on 26 October 2026 RustSec published 13 against one compression crate, and 0.2.3 shipped with them under a dated, reviewed exception.
